## My school board decision and endorsements

On July 20, 2026, I chose to withdraw from the school board race. I endorse Phil Kim, Alida Fisher, and Ryan Hazelton. This is my personal view. The names appear in page order only. They are not ranked or ballot advice.

On October 14, the City’s candidate page listed eight people for three Board seats. It did not list my name. The page does not say why. It does not confirm that the City accepted my withdrawal or changed the ballot. Use the official links below to check your own ballot and current rules.

## Three candidates. Three reasons.

- Phil Kim — I endorse Phil for strong teaching, help for students who need it, clear Board oversight, and sound budgets. His public answers call for data to find student needs, help for teachers, and spending that matches student goals. These are his plans. They are not proof of results.
- Alida Fisher — I endorse Alida for her work with students who need special education. She calls for early help, fair access, and strong support for students and families. These are her views. They do not prove results.
- Ryan Hazelton — I endorse Ryan for his focus on family access, keeping teachers in our schools, and plans that work. He backs the school plan now in place and wants fair access and strong local schools. These are his views, not proof of results.

## What they said in common

All three chose “Strongly Agree” on the same five questions in a survey by SF Parents Action. The questions covered fairness, student results, reading and math, the Board’s role, and school budgets. This shows what they said on those questions. It does not prove they share a plan or will get the same results. The files do not show when they sent their answers.

## Where their answers differ

### School assignment

Ryan wants the district to move ahead with its school plan. He says do not delay when it can be avoided. He also wants fair access and strong local schools. Phil calls for data and a careful plan. Alida says a new plan must give each student fair access to language classes, special education, STEM, and inclusive programs. Their answers differ on pace and safeguards. This does not show that Phil or Alida oppose change.

### Possible school mergers

Phil says any plan to merge schools needs clear data, public input, and a plan to help current schools. Alida supports careful change and says it may not save money. Ryan’s written answer leaves this choice open until the district acts on school assignment and enrollment. A later forum report says he answered “no” in a quick yes or no round. Those answers came from two events. Read both before you decide what they mean.
